Automating business processes means letting computers and software carry out certain tasks instead of staff members. Repetitive, time-consuming work is done faster and with fewer errors this way, while your people keep capacity for higher-value work. In this article you will read what process automation actually involves, which benefits it delivers, which processes lend themselves best to it and how to start an automation project sensibly.

## What does automating business processes mean?

Automating business processes means that software carries out tasks that employees would otherwise do manually: retyping data between systems, preparing invoices, sending reminders or processing orders. The aim is to make repetitive, time-consuming work faster, more efficient and less error-prone, so your team gains room for work that genuinely requires attention.

In practice, automation often starts at the places where systems stand apart from each other. Every department works in its own environment, think of an accounting package, an ERP and loose spreadsheets, and employees form the manual link between them. It is precisely those manual links that are slow and error-prone.

Salesforce lends itself well as a foundation for automation, because customer, order and service data already come together there. By connecting your system landscape to Salesforce, data synchronises automatically and retyping disappears. On our [solutions page](/en/oplossingen) you can see which common challenges, from disconnected systems to time-consuming invoicing, can be solved this way.

## Which benefits does process automation deliver?

Process automation delivers four key benefits: greater speed and accuracy because manual tasks disappear, lower costs through fewer labour hours and less error correction, real-time data for better-informed decisions, and time flowing back to your team for strategic initiatives and personal customer contact.

Spelled out, that means:

- **Speed and accuracy**: fewer manual tasks, so processes run faster and with fewer errors.
- **Lower costs**: less labour cost for repetitive work and less time lost to correcting mistakes.
- **Real-time data**: dashboards and reports on up-to-date figures, for faster and better-informed decisions.
- **Time back**: room for strategic initiatives and for the work where your people make the difference.

There is also a less tangible but very real benefit: motivation. Repetitive work costs not only time but also job satisfaction. Employees who retype data day in, day out are doing work a system can do better. Automation gives their role more substance.

## Which processes qualify first?

The best candidates for automation are processes that are repetitive, predictable and high-volume: invoicing, onboarding new customers, data entry across multiple systems and sending reminders. The more often a process repeats itself in the same form, the greater the gain from automating it.

Think, for example, of:

1. **Invoicing and accounts receivable**: generating, sending and following up invoices with automatic payment reminders.
2. **Customer and order onboarding**: processing new customers and orders across all systems without manual entry.
3. **Data entry and data enrichment**: retrieving and maintaining company data automatically instead of copying it by hand.
4. **Workflows and document generation**: creating quotes, contracts and other documents automatically from your CRM data.

Invoicing is a prime candidate as a starting point: preparation time drops and errors disappear, while the result is immediately visible in the figures. Service-oriented processes also lend themselves well, especially when customer queries keep taking the same form.

## How do you automate invoicing and accounts receivable?

You automate invoicing in Salesforce with a solution such as our [Billing Platform](/en/producten/billing-module): invoices are generated automatically as PDFs based on product type, language and location, subscriptions are activated from closed deals, and payment reminders go out automatically at the right moments.

The chain works from deal to payment. When an opportunity is closed-won, the subscription is activated automatically, the right invoice is created in the right language and template, and via the Stripe integration the amount is collected using the customer's preferred payment method. All the figures, from Monthly Recurring Revenue to outstanding items, appear live on dashboards.

For the finance team this means less manual work and faster payment. Reminders send themselves, revenue is attributed cleanly to the right month using start and end dates on invoice lines, and the accounts stay in sync via ERP integrations.

## How do you keep your customer data clean and current?

You keep data clean automatically with a tool such as [Datasolver](/en/producten/kvk-integratie): company details are retrieved directly from the Dutch Chamber of Commerce register, EU VAT numbers are validated via VIES, and automatic periodic checks flag any changes. Your database stays current without manual searching or retyping.

This is an often underestimated form of process automation. Polluted data undermines every other automated process: an invoice sent to an outdated address or carrying an invalid VAT number still results in manual work. Anyone automating would therefore do well to secure data quality first.

Datasolver enriches records with a single click with trade names, SBI codes, addresses and branch details, among other things, and looks up Peppol identification numbers without leaving Salesforce. That also prepares you for the mandatory e-invoicing that is coming across Europe.

## Where do you start with an automation project?

You start by defining the goal: which process do you want to speed up and what should it deliver? Then choose the right technology, paying attention to scalability and security, and plan the training of your team. That way, automation becomes a lasting success rather than a one-off intervention.

Three points of attention make the difference:

1. **Goal and yardstick**: establish beforehand what the process costs today and what the automation should deliver, so the result is measurable.
2. **Technology choice**: pay attention to scalability and security, so the solution grows with your organisation and meets your requirements.
3. **Training and adoption**: bring your team along in the new way of working; an automated process that nobody trusts or uses delivers nothing.

If you want to go a step further, AI agents are the next phase: with [Agentforce](/en/diensten/agentforce) you let autonomous agents carry out routine tasks independently, such as handling cases and qualifying leads. Process automation and AI reinforce each other here: the better your processes and data are organised, the more an AI agent can do with them.

Well-executed process automation leads to smoother-running processes, better adherence to SLAs and fewer customer queries. Employees spend their time on higher-value work, decisions are taken on real-time data, and the error-proneness of manual entry disappears from the chain.

That result does not come from one big intervention, but from starting deliberately with the process that offers the greatest gain and building out from there. The PayPlaza example shows how automation across sales, service, logistics and the back office ultimately speeds up the entire organisation.
