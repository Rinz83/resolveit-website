The software matters, but your implementation partner largely determines the success of your CRM project. A good partner helps with system selection, sets the system up correctly, trains your staff and remains involved after go-live. In this article you will read what a CRM partner actually does for you, what to look out for when choosing one and which questions to ask in an introductory meeting.

## Why is the choice of your CRM partner so decisive?

The choice of your CRM partner is decisive because the same software, in different hands, produces a completely different result. A partner translates your processes into a workable set-up, builds support among your staff and prevents unnecessary custom development. Without that translation, even the best CRM system remains an empty database.

A CRM project touches almost every department: sales, service, marketing and often the accounts as well. The set-up must follow how your organisation works, not the other way round. That calls for a partner who listens and asks questions first, and only then starts building.

The partner also sets the pace and quality of adoption. A system that is technically sound but used by no one delivers nothing. Our experience across more than 340 projects is that adoption is not an afterthought: it has to be built into the [implementation](/en/diensten/implementatie) from day one.

## What does a good CRM partner do for you?

A good CRM partner guides the entire journey: from system selection and implementation to training, strategic advice and maintenance. The partner ensures the system is set up correctly, that your staff can work with it and that the environment stays current with new versions and developments.

In concrete terms, you may expect the following roles from a CRM partner:

- **System selection**: helps determine which CRM aligns with your needs and strategic goals.
- **Implementation**: guides the full roll-out and ensures the system is set up correctly.
- **Training**: trains your staff for maximum adoption.
- **Strategic sounding board**: thinks along about your CRM strategy and the buy-in among staff.
- **Maintenance**: keeps your system up to date with the latest versions and developments.

Bear in mind that not every partner takes each of these roles equally seriously. Some agencies deliver and leave. So ask explicitly what the collaboration looks like after go-live, for example in the form of [managed services](/en/diensten/managed-services) or an ongoing relationship with fixed consultants.

## What should you look out for when choosing a CRM partner?

When choosing a CRM partner, look above all at current knowledge, proven results, independence and the way of working together. An experienced partner is happy to share successful examples from previous clients, speaks plain language and makes clear agreements about what you can and cannot expect.

A practical checklist for your selection:

1. **Current knowledge**: does the partner have certified consultants who genuinely keep up with the platform?
2. **Proven results**: are there concrete customer cases with measurable outcomes, rather than just logos?
3. **Independence**: does the partner advise in your interest, or mainly out of a desire to sell licences and hours?
4. **Who does the work**: are you speaking to the senior consultant who will be on your project, or to an account manager?
5. **Approach to custom work**: does the partner choose existing products where possible, or build everything bespoke?
6. **Knowledge transfer**: are documentation and training a standard part of every delivery?
7. **Aftercare**: is there a clear story for maintenance and further development after go-live?

When assessing proven results, do not stop at the references: probe the details. What was the starting situation, what was built and what did it measurably deliver? Our own [customer cases](/en/cases) are structured that way for exactly this reason.

## Which questions should you ask in an introductory meeting?

In an introductory meeting, ask questions that reveal how the partner works: who will be on your project, how adoption is safeguarded, how custom work is handled and what happens after go-live. The answers tell you more than any proposal or presentation.

A few questions that work well in practice:

- Who carries out the work, and am I speaking to that person now?
- Can you show me a comparable project, including the results?
- How do you make sure our staff will actually use the system?
- When do you recommend an existing product, and when custom development?
- What does the collaboration look like after go-live?
- What do you expect from us as the client?

That last question matters more than it seems. CRM success calls for collaboration: your staff must start working together more efficiently, with clear agreements on how the system is used. A partner who asks nothing of you is probably not taking the project seriously enough.

## How do you recognise independent advice?

You recognise independent advice by a partner who also dares to advise against something. Whoever says honestly what does not work, what is superfluous or what you could better handle yourself, is advising in your interest. A partner with a hidden agenda, by contrast, almost always steers towards more licences, more custom work and more hours.

A good test is how the engagement starts. Does the partner begin with an analysis of your situation, or go straight to a quotation? We, for example, start with a [free assessment](/en/diensten/consultancy) of the existing environment, with quick wins first and only then a roadmap. That way you know exactly where you stand before deciding anything, and you can pick up the recommendations yourself if you prefer.

Independence also means: no unnecessary custom development. If a proven product exists for your requirement, it is almost always cheaper and easier to maintain than a solution built especially for you. So always ask a partner which existing products were considered before custom work was proposed.

## What may you expect after go-live?

After go-live, you may expect knowledge transfer, maintenance and further development from a good partner. Documentation and training are part of the delivery, so your team can work with the system independently. In addition, a good partner remains available for questions, new requirements and the ongoing development of your environment.

Go-live is not the end of the project but the beginning of actual use. Your organisation changes, and your CRM must move with it: new processes, new integrations, new employees who need training. So choose a partner geared towards long-term collaboration rather than one-off projects.

Pay attention to the balance between involvement and dependency. Knowledge transfer belongs in every delivery, precisely so that you are not dependent on your partner. A working CRM ultimately gives you insight into all customer touchpoints and customer needs, and that is what makes selling more efficient. The partner is the means to that end, not the end itself.
